Portuguese "embaraçada" = embarrassed
Spanish "embarazada" = pregnant

Both from Latin *barra (obstacle) via Vulgar Latin barrāre (to obstruct). Entered Romance in the 13th-14th c. as imbarazzare (Italian), embaraçar (Portuguese).

French borrowed in the 1580s, English in the 1670s. All kept the "obstruct → feel awkward" route.

Only Spanish diverged: by the 16th c., embarazada specialized to "pregnant". Spanish already had avergonzada for "embarrassed".
